The Dusk Gap

Formal verification of automatic emergency braking across illuminations that FMVSS 127 does not test. WMU AD Assurance Lab, simulated in CARLA on Town01.

A policy that passes both regulatory test points 10/10 fails 0 out of 10 at three dusk illuminations between them. The certificate named those illuminations without simulating, and the verdicts were committed to version control before any vehicle moved.

P_pts — trained on the regulatory test points only P_cont — trained across the illumination continuum

Certified margin, per sub-interval

Lower bound on commanded deceleration, as a multiple of the brake decision threshold. At or above 1.0 the policy is certified to brake in time for every illumination in that sub-interval. Below it, it is not.

Driven outcome, ten runs per sub-interval

Closed-loop runs at each sub-interval's midpoint illumination, a rendered condition rather than an interpolation. Certified sub-intervals were driven too, so the chart can distinguish a working certificate from one that flags everything.

What this does and does not establish
  • The control is what makes it mean something. The two policies share architecture, training recipe and sample count. Only the illumination levels they saw differ, so the gap is attributable to how that axis was sampled.
  • Every disagreement is conservative. Agreement was 10/11 and 9/11; in each miss the verifier called unsafe something that drove cleanly. Nothing was certified and then failed.
  • The bound tracks severity, not just a threshold. A margin of 0.99 drove 6/10. Deeply negative margins drove 0/10. Comfortable margins drove 10/10.
  • Both policies fail the deepest twilight. Continuum training buys margin in the shoulder and little at the horizon, where illumination changes fastest and the disturbance family is hardest to construct.
  • Scope. Simulation only, one map, one speed, one vehicle, camera only, stationary lead vehicle. This is a subset of FMVSS 127 scenarios and is not a compliance demonstration.
