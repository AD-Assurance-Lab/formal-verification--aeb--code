The dusk gap

A camera-only AEB policy on the stopped lead vehicle scenario, certified over every illumination between two mandated test conditions, then driven at the illuminations the certificate names. Required braking range 52.0 ft; 25 poses inside it; 13 sub-intervals of sun altitude.

P_pts passes both regulatory lighting conditions and is falsified across 17.2° of sun altitude between them. P_cont, identical but for how the illumination axis was sampled, is falsified across 0.0°.

Certified margin, per sub-interval

Lower bound on commanded deceleration, as a multiple of the brake decision threshold. At or above 1.0 the policy is certified to brake in time for every illumination in that sub-interval. Below it, it is not.

Driven outcome, ten runs per sub-interval

Closed-loop runs at each sub-interval's midpoint illumination, a rendered condition rather than an interpolation. Certified sub-intervals were driven too, so the chart can distinguish a working certificate from one that flags everything.

What this does and does not establish
  • The control is what makes it mean something. The two policies share architecture, training recipe and sample count. Only the illumination levels they saw differ, so the gap is attributable to how that axis was sampled.
  • Certificate against driving: P_pts 10/13, P_cont 13/13 sub-intervals agree. Nothing was certified and then failed.
  • 1 sub-interval is declared uncovered (+0.241° to +0.000°). The blend cannot represent the horizon at any width, so a bound there quantifies over images the simulator would never render. Drawn hatched, and excluded from every count on this page.
  • Scope. Simulation only, one map, one site, one speed, one vehicle, camera only. This is a subset of FMVSS 127 scenarios and is not a compliance demonstration.
